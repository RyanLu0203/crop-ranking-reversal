# GOAL-16 pre-redesign visual audit

## Scope and frozen baseline

- Baseline: commit `4088e6223c3d6ac13841c2cacc90181e21741a86`.
- Figures inspected: main Figures 1--6 and Supplementary Figures S1--S4.
- Views inspected: full-page PDF render, 183 mm at 300 dpi, 89 mm at 300 dpi,
  grayscale, simulated deuteranopia and simulated protanopia.
- Reproduction command: `python scripts/build_goal16_baseline_visual_audit.py`
  using the repository Python environment.
- Evidence package: `audits/goal16_visual_baseline/`; its manifest records the
  exact source hashes and its `SHA256SUMS.txt` covers every audit render.

This is a pre-modification audit. No figure-generating source or committed
figure export was changed before the observations below were frozen.

## Global findings

1. The six-colour card exists in configuration, but the rendered system does
   not communicate a stable semantic mapping. Pale derived fills dominate,
   promoted and descriptive evidence can look similar, and several marks read
   as generic blue/teal Matplotlib output.
2. Figures alternate among flow charts, sparse one-dimensional diagrams,
   stacked bars, forest plots, heatmaps and text cards without a common axis,
   annotation or evidence-status grammar.
3. At 89 mm, secondary annotations below about 5.5 pt, repeated treatment codes,
   and multi-item legends become decoding tasks. No gross clipping is visible,
   but practical legibility fails in several panels.
4. Grayscale retains most geometric distinctions, but crop identity collapses
   in stacked compositions and promoted/adverse status sometimes depends on
   tone alone. Deuteranopia and protanopia preserve the major teal/violet split,
   but line and fill similarity remains too high where no marker or hatch is
   provided.
5. Main figures mix evidence classes. In particular, failed E3 and E5 evidence
   sits beside E2 and E6 positive results. This weakens hierarchy even though
   captions state the boundary.
6. The empirical figure ignores geography despite state-level data and uses a
   generic heatmap/bar combination for spatial, temporal and aggregation
   questions.
