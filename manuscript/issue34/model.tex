\section{Stochastic crop-planning model}

\subsection{Scores, margins and joint uncertainty}

Let \(i=1,\ldots,n\) index crops and \(x_i\) denote planted area. A
pre-decision historical relative-yield performance index \(s_i\) induces a
crop order. The index is external to the optimization model; no cardinal
transformation of \(s\) is used as a margin.
For state \(\omega\), stochastic per-acre margin and portfolio profit are
\begin{equation}
\widetilde\pi_i(\omega)=\widetilde P_i(\omega)\widetilde
Y_i(\omega)-\widetilde C_i(\omega),\qquad
\widetilde\Pi(x,\omega)=\sum_i x_i\widetilde\pi_i(\omega).
\end{equation}
The joint distribution includes within-crop price--yield dependence and
cross-crop co-movement. Let \(\mu_i=\E[\widetilde\pi_i]\) and define loss
\(L(x,\omega)=-\widetilde\Pi(x,\omega)\). A larger loss-CVaR is worse; because
profitable portfolios can have negative loss-CVaR, ``more negative'' means
safer under this convention.

\subsection{Operational feasibility}

The operational set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal X=\left\{x\in\R_+^n:
\ind^\top x\le A,\quad c^\top x\le B,\quad
Rx\le r,\quad Kx\ge k,\quad Gx\le h,\quad
\ell\le x\le u\right\}.
\label{eq:operations}
\end{equation}
Here \(A\) is available land, \(c\) and \(B\) describe pre-plant cash
requirements, \(R\) contains rotation caps, \(K\) contains contract minimums,
and \(G\) contains shared labour, equipment, irrigation or storage rows. Idle
land is \(A-\ind^\top x\) and is reported even when it is not assigned a
separate variable. Together, the constraints define the feasible planting
decisions before acreage is optimized.

\subsection{Heuristic-policy projection}

Let \(x^h\) be a raw score-proportional, score-winner or equal-share
prescription. Its principal comparison policy is the unique Euclidean
projection
\begin{equation}
\widehat x^{\,h}_2
=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{x\in\mathcal X_h}
\frac12\lVert x-x^h\rVert_2^2,
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal X_h\) imposes \(\ind^\top x=A\), the crop bounds, budget,
rotation, contract, labour and equipment constraints in
Eq.~\ref{eq:operations}. Idle land is therefore excluded from the comparison
set. The loss-CVaR ceiling is not part of the projection; loss-CVaR and risk
feasibility are evaluated after projection. Strict convexity gives a unique
principal projection. An unweighted \(L^1\) projection provides a sensitivity
comparison; on a non-unique \(L^1\)-optimal face, a fixed crop-order weighted
linear objective selects one allocation deterministically.

\subsection{Loss-CVaR ceiling}

For confidence level \(\alpha\),
\begin{equation}
r_\alpha(x)=\CVaR_\alpha[L(x)]
=\min_{v\in\R}\left\{v+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
\E[(L(x)-v)_+]\right\}.
\end{equation}
The producer solves
\begin{equation}
\max_{x\in\mathcal X}\ \mu^\top x
\quad\text{subject to}\quad r_\alpha(x)\le\kappa.
\label{eq:canonical}
\end{equation}
For scenarios \(m=1,\ldots,M\) with probabilities \(w_m\), free threshold
\(v\) and excess variables \(q_m\ge0\), the risk restriction is
\begin{align}
v+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\sum_m w_mq_m&\le\kappa,\\
q_m&\ge-\pi_m^\top x-v.
\end{align}
The resulting programme is linear. The risk ceiling is indexed by
\(\rho\in[0,1]\):
\begin{equation}
\kappa(\rho)=\kappa_{\min}+\rho
\left(\kappa_{\mathrm{EP}}-\kappa_{\min}\right),
\label{eq:riskpath}
\end{equation}
where \(\kappa_{\min}\) is minimum attainable loss-CVaR and
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{EP}}\) is the loss-CVaR of the unconstrained
expected-profit endpoint. This makes risk tolerance comparable across joint
laws without transferring an arbitrary dollar ceiling.

\subsection{Reversal and optimal faces}

\begin{definition}[Pairwise, complete rank and strong exclusion reversal]
For a pair \(s_i>s_j\), an allocation reverses the ranking if \(x_i<x_j\).
A complete rank reversal occurs when the highest-ranked crop has less area
than every lower-ranked crop. A strong exclusion reversal requires
\(s_i>s_j\) and \(x_i=0<x_j\).
\end{definition}

Positive crop lower bounds preclude strong exclusion reversal in the principal
specification. Sensitivity analyses evaluate this outcome after setting either
all lower bounds or only the highest-ranked crop's lower bound to zero.

Let
\[
\mathcal S_\kappa=\argmax\{\mu^\top x:x\in\mathcal X,\,
r_\alpha(x)\le\kappa\}
\]
be the complete optimal set. Reversal is \emph{possible} if it occurs at some
\(x\in\mathcal S_\kappa\), \emph{universal} if it occurs at every such point,
and \emph{selected} if it occurs at the point chosen by a specified
deterministic selector. Pairwise classification follows the minimum and
maximum of \(x_j-x_i\) on the objective-equivalent face; one optimal vertex
does not establish universal reversal. Ordering and operational zero use numerical
tolerances of \(10^{-4}\) normalized land share; sensitivity spans
\(10^{-8}\) to \(10^{-2}\).

\subsection{Full stationarity system}

Let nonnegative multipliers
\(\eta,\nu^A,\nu^B,\nu^R,\nu^K,\nu^G,\nu^\ell,\nu^u\)
correspond to loss-CVaR, land, budget, rotation, sign-converted contract,
shared capacity, lower bounds and upper bounds. For a loss-CVaR subgradient
\(d\in\partial r_\alpha(x^*)\), acreage stationarity is
\begin{equation}
-\mu+\eta d+\nu^A\ind+\nu^B c+R^\top\nu^R-K^\top\nu^K+
G^\top\nu^G-\nu^\ell+\nu^u=0.
\label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
Pairwise subtraction cancels the common land multiplier but retains budget,
shared-resource and crop-bound pressures. Equation~\ref{eq:kkt} is a marginal
balance rather than an acreage-ordering formula. The finite-scenario KKT
system also includes stationarity for \(v\) and every \(q_m\), including
fractional tail weights when the loss distribution has atoms. These
conditions provide the optimality basis for the reversal frontier developed
next.
