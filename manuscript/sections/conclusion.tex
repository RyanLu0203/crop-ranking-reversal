\section{Conclusion}

Crop rankings and land allocations are different mathematical objects. A set-valued stochastic programme shows why: cardinal margins, joint uncertainty, operational feasibility and optimizer selection all intervene between an order and an acreage vector. Operational constraints can force universal reversal, while dependence and information claims require stronger conditions than a scalar index or a general appeal to flexibility.

The Stage II evidence supplies positive results without erasing boundaries. E2 identifies operationally induced reversal in all treated cells, and E6 shows positive, null and substitutive information--flexibility interactions. Four other experiments remain adverse because their precision gates fail. Admitted state data reveal concurrent ranking--acreage inversions but no robust lagged share response and no aggregation-invariant rate. Ranking reversal is therefore demonstrable under explicit mechanisms and empirically visible as a descriptive pattern, but its field mechanism must be measured rather than inferred from disagreement alone.
