\section{Definitions and complete model notation}

Pairwise reversal is \(s_i>s_j,\ x_i<x_j\). Complete rank reversal means that
the top-ranked crop receives less than every lower-ranked crop. Strong
exclusion reversal requires \(s_i>s_j,\ x_i=0<x_j\). These definitions
separate an allocation-order reversal from exclusion of the higher-ranked
crop.

\section{Full finite-scenario linear programme}

For scenario probabilities \(w_m>0\) summing to one, the finite-scenario
formulation is
\begin{align}
\max_{x,v,q}\quad & \mu^\top x\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\ind^\top x\le A,\\
&c^\top x\le B,\\
&Rx\le r,\qquad -Kx\le-k,\qquad Gx\le h,\\
&\ell\le x\le u,\\
&v+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\sum_{m=1}^M w_mq_m\le\kappa,\\
&-\pi_m^\top x-v-q_m\le0,\qquad m=1,\ldots,M,\\
&q_m\ge0,\qquad v\in\R.
\end{align}
The objective contains expected profit only; risk and operational effects
enter through the explicit constraints.

\subsection{Heuristic-policy comparison set and projection}

For raw heuristic \(x^h\), the principal comparison allocation is
\[
\widehat x^{\,h}_2
=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_x\frac12\lVert x-x^h\rVert_2^2
\]
subject to \(\ind^\top x=1\), the crop lower and upper bounds, operating
budget, corn rotation cap, soybean contract minimum, planting-labour capacity
and harvest-equipment capacity. Idle land is not allowed. The loss-CVaR ceiling is
not included in this projection set; projected policies are evaluated against
it afterwards. The unweighted objective is strictly convex, so the Euclidean
projection is unique. SLSQP uses objective tolerance \(10^{-10}\), followed by
an independent \(10^{-8}\) feasibility check.

The sensitivity projection minimizes \(\lVert x-x^h\rVert_1\) over the same
set by HiGHS. A second linear programme selects one point on a non-unique
\(L^1\)-optimal face by minimizing crop-order weights \((1,2,3)^\top x\)
within \(10^{-10}\) of the minimum distance. This deterministic rule affects
only benchmark comparison policies, not any optimized model solution.

\subsection{KKT equations}

Let \(\eta\) be the loss-CVaR ceiling multiplier, \(t_m\) the multiplier for
scenario excess row \(m\), and \(z_m\) the multiplier on \(q_m\ge0\). Together
with the operational multipliers defined in the main text, stationarity is
\begin{align}
0={}&-\mu+\nu^A\ind+\nu^B c+R^\top\nu^R-K^\top\nu^K+
G^\top\nu^G-\nu^\ell+\nu^u-\sum_mt_m\pi_m,\\
0={}&\eta-\sum_mt_m,\\
0={}&\frac{\eta w_m}{1-\alpha}-t_m-z_m,\qquad m=1,\ldots,M.
\end{align}
All multipliers associated with inequality constraints are nonnegative.
Because \(v\) is unrestricted in sign, stationarity with respect to \(v\)
yields the equality in Eq.~(10). Complementarity requires each multiplier
times its primal slack to be zero. The scenario equations allow fractional
mass at the VaR threshold and are valid for discrete scenario distributions.

\section{Proofs}

\subsection{Proof of the restricted reversal theorem}

Let \(z=x_1\) and \(x_2=A-z\). Expected profit is
\[
\mu_1z+\mu_2(A-z)=\mu_2A+(\mu_1-\mu_2)z.
\]
Because \(\mu_1>\mu_2\), this function is strictly increasing in \(z\).
Loss-CVaR is convex and continuous in the portfolio weights. Its sublevel set
intersected with the compact operational interval \(I\) is therefore a compact
interval \(\mathcal K_{\theta,\kappa}\). A strictly increasing linear
objective has the unique maximizer \(\sup\mathcal K_{\theta,\kappa}\).

The pair reverses when \(z<A-z\), equivalently \(z<A/2\). Hence reversal holds
if and only if the right endpoint of the entire risk-feasible interval lies
below \(A/2\), which is equivalent to no feasible point on
\([A/2,\,\overline z]\). If risk is nondecreasing on that upper half, the
minimum upper-half risk occurs at \(A/2\), so the upper half is infeasible if
and only if \(r_\theta(A/2)>\kappa\). Existence of a feasible lower-half point
ensures that this inequality indicates reversal rather than infeasibility.

If \(\theta\mapsto r_\theta(A/2)\) is continuous, strictly increasing and
crosses \(\kappa\), the intermediate-value theorem gives a unique
\(\theta^*\). If strictness is removed, an interval of thresholds can result.
If monotonicity is removed, the reversal set can be disconnected. If crossing
is removed, a threshold need not exist.

\subsection{Proof of complete optimality}

The finite-scenario problem is a bounded linear programme whenever
\(\mathcal X\) is nonempty and bounded. Linear-programming strong duality
implies that primal and dual feasibility plus equality of the objective values
are necessary and sufficient. Expanding equality of values across all
inequality rows yields stationarity and complementarity. The equations above
therefore form a complete certificate.

For the population convex formulation, write the Lagrangian using loss-CVaR
as a convex function. Under Slater's condition or another convex constraint
qualification, KKT conditions are necessary; convexity makes them sufficient.
Pairwise subtraction of acreage stationarity cancels \(\nu^A\) but leaves
budget, rotation, contract, shared-resource and bound terms. No algebraic
operation on this local equality recovers an acreage ordering without the
global feasible geometry.

\subsection{Proof of named-family ordering}

For integrable random losses, loss-CVaR is a law-invariant coherent risk measure and
is monotone under convex order. Thus
\(L_{\theta_1}(x)\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}L_{\theta_2}(x)\) implies the stated
loss-CVaR inequality at each \(x\). Pointwise inequality gives containment of the
two risk sublevel sets. Maximizing the same expected-profit objective over
nested sets gives the optimal-value order. Coordinate monotonicity does not
follow because the supporting face can change when the feasible set contracts.

\subsection{Proofs for information and flexibility}

In a posterior-separable model, or when the shared ex-ante risk restriction is
nonbinding, any no-information action defines a constant signal-contingent policy. If the
constant policy is feasible, optimization after observing a signal cannot
lower value. When one common action is posterior-optimal for every signal, the
constant policy also attains the informed optimum, proving zero value. If
unique posterior optima differ on events with positive probability and at
least one difference is strict, expected improvement is positive. This
posterior-optimum strictness step is not applied when contingent actions share
a binding ex-ante loss-CVaR budget.

Increasing differences and nested action sets yield monotone posterior actions
under standard monotone comparative statics. Strictness on a positive-
probability event makes the information gain larger at the higher acreage-
flexibility level. This is the conditional complementarity result.

For buffering, at \(\phi=1\) all state margins equal \(\bar\pi\); posterior
optimization problems are identical and the common-action condition gives
zero information value. If information value is positive at \(\phi=0\) and
continuous along the contraction path, it must decline somewhere. Those
intervals are substitutive. Total optimized value may still rise with
\(\phi\).

For the complete shared ex-ante loss-CVaR numerical programme, interaction is classified
without extending that theorem:
\[
\Delta_{\xi,\phi}V=[V(\xi_2,\phi_2)-V(\xi_1,\phi_2)]
-[V(\xi_2,\phi_1)-V(\xi_1,\phi_1)].
\]
The sign is computed on each adjacent grid rectangle.

\section{Phase-diagram summary}

The mechanism-isolation analysis covers every evaluated parameter
combination. In the margin layer, top-ranked winter wheat has the lowest
public-data mean margin and
the expected-profit endpoint already has a complete rank reversal; loss-CVaR
raises its share. In the risk layer, soybean has the higher index and mean
margin, while
a mean-preserving 10\% downside event of \(\$\,\RiskShock\) per acre produces
the loose-to-tight allocation crossing reported in the main text. Across the
probability-by-magnitude map, \RiskShockCrossingCells{} of the
\RiskShockCells{} stress combinations produce an allocation crossing,
\RiskShockNoCrossingCells{} remain feasible without a crossing, and one is
infeasible. In the
operational layer, the baseline calibrated constraint sequence remains
non-reversing before the soybean rotation-cap path is evaluated; the first
crossing is at \OperationalCrossingCap. These controlled paths demonstrate
mechanisms rather than their population frequency.

Across all \PhaseCells{} principal cells, selected pairwise reversal occurs in
\PhaseReversal{} and selected complete rank reversal in \PhaseComplete.
Strong exclusion is structurally inadmissible because every crop has a
positive lower bound. Complete family-by-\(\tau\) tables report all three
ranked-pair face extrema and possible, universal and selected classifications
for every parameter combination.

\section{Robustness and uncertainty}

The one-at-a-time robustness analysis reports selected pairwise and selected
complete rank reversal classifications separately for confidence level,
constraint regime, dependence family and parameter, marginal law, sample
window, scenario count, random seed and solver. Its strong classification is
structurally unavailable because all crop lower bounds are positive. In the
all-zero and highest-ranked-crop-zero phases, strong exclusion reversal remains
absent for every zero tolerance from \(10^{-8}\) to \(10^{-2}\); pairwise and
complete rank reversal counts change only at the coarser ordering tolerances.

Sixty-four historical bootstrap replications produce mean shares 0.277 for
corn, 0.527 for soybean and 0.196 for winter wheat. Their 95\% percentile
intervals are \([0.125,\,0.368]\), \([0.258,\,0.650]\) and
\([0.070,\,0.404]\). The binary reversal frequency across historical resamples
is treated separately. Selected pairwise reversal occurs in
\BootstrapReversalCount{} of 64
resamples. Its exact Clopper--Pearson binomial interval is
\([\BootstrapProbabilityLow,\,\BootstrapProbabilityHigh]\). The interval describes
the frequency among historical resamples, not a farm-population probability.
The first-reversal
index mean is \BootstrapFirstMean{} with interval
\([\BootstrapFirstLow,\,\BootstrapFirstHigh]\). The broad boundary interval is
consistent with an eight-year calibration; simulated scenarios do not add
independent historical information.

\section{Information--flexibility details}

The state split contains four low and four high relative-yield-advantage
years. Signal accuracy takes six values and flexibility takes six values on
each path, producing 72 cells. Adjacent-grid classifications are
\InfoPositiveCells{} positive cross-differences, \InfoNegativeCells{} negative
cross-differences, \InfoZeroCells{} zero-information and
\InfoBoundaryCells{} zero/boundary. Every signal-contingent problem is
feasible and the minimum computed value of information differs from zero only
at floating-point scale.

At perfect signal accuracy, post-signal acreage reallocation raises
information value monotonically from zero at no adjustable acreage to
\(\$\,\MaxInformationValue\) at full adjustment. Under shock buffering, the
corresponding information value declines toward zero as the state margins are
contracted to a common vector. This comparison is about interaction; buffering
can still increase the no-information optimized value.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The Kansas calibration has eight effective annual observations. It
cannot estimate extreme-tail dependence reliably.
\item State yield combined with national price and cost is not a farm-level
profit series.
\item Copula parameters and shared-capacity limits are controlled stress
values, not causal estimates.
\item The model is single-period and linear. It omits fixed charges,
endogenous prices, spatial spillovers and explicit multi-year rotation state.
\item Historical bootstrap replications quantify sensitivity to the short
calibration record but do not create independent empirical observations.
\item The information state is a transparent annual proxy, not a deployed
forecast product.
\item The external panel is aggregate and descriptive; it cannot identify the
full model's active mechanism or welfare.
\end{enumerate}
