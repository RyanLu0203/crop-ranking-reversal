\section{Discussion}

The central result is that ranking reversal is not one mechanism. It is a relation between an ordinal statement and a cardinal allocation drawn from a constrained optimal set. Exact theory identifies when ordinal information is insufficient and when feasibility alone forces reversal. Precision-qualified experiments then demonstrate two positive mechanism classes: operational interventions can overturn a fixed ranking, and the value interaction between information and flexibility changes sign with the actions that flexibility makes available.

This mechanism view improves on both extremes of interpretation. Treating every score--acreage mismatch as an error ignores objectives, constraints and uncertainty. Treating every mismatch as uninformative misses controlled cases in which the intervention and optimal face are known. E2 identifies operational displacement because the constraint intervention is assigned inside a closed simulation. E6 identifies actionability because signal and action-set contrasts are factorial and invariant checks pass. Neither result makes those mechanisms empirically prevalent.

The failed experiments are scientifically informative. E1, E3, E4 and E5 show that deterministic reproduction, solver agreement and visually suggestive contrasts do not guarantee registered precision. They remain nonheadline and cannot support a unique dependence threshold, a general CVaR effect or a copula-misspecification penalty. Retaining all failures and infeasible rows makes the evidence rule inspectable rather than retrospective.

The empirical evidence establishes a different layer. Concurrent rank--acreage inversion is common in the complete-case state panel, yet it is definition-sensitive; lagged share-change contrasts include zero; and the national comparison ranges from null to two-thirds depending on definition, with relative-yield ties. The optimizing mechanism is not identified. In particular, the data provide no private constraints or objectives, and they do not identify CVaR preferences, copula causality, welfare, optimal acreage or why an individual state-year differs.

Several limitations follow. The empirical panel covers three crops and three years and uses national price and cost inputs to standardize accounting. Observed acreage embeds unobserved expectations, policies, contracts, rotations and adjustment costs. The baseline model is linear and single-period, omitting fixed costs, endogenous prices, spatial interaction and dynamic rotations. The promoted simulations demonstrate existence and sign heterogeneity within declared domains, not population frequencies. Their parameters are controlled design values rather than farm estimates.

The practical implication is procedural. A ranking system intended to guide acreage should report the cardinal mapping from scores to payoffs, the provenance of operational restrictions, the joint uncertainty model, the risk convention, the optimizer-selection rule and sensitivity to plausible alternatives. A signal is valuable only if it can alter a feasible action, and added flexibility can complement, substitute for, or be irrelevant to that signal. When decision inputs are unavailable, rank--acreage comparisons should be labelled descriptive rather than converted into efficiency or compliance judgments.
