\begin{abstract}
Crop recommendation systems rank crops using agronomic or predicted
performance, whereas producers allocate land under uncertain margins, joint
downside risk and operational restrictions. We ask when these two orders
reverse and how diversification and climate information alter the
resulting exposure. We formulate a stochastic crop-planning programme with
uncertain price, yield and cost; land, budget, rotation, contract, labour and
equipment constraints; and a loss-CVaR ceiling. A restricted two-crop theorem
gives a necessary-and-sufficient reversal frontier: when the higher-ranked crop
also has the higher expected margin, reversal occurs exactly when no
risk-feasible allocation remains on the upper half of its feasible share
interval. The result is family-specific and need not reduce to a universal
tail-dependence threshold. A public-data-calibrated Kansas experiment uses
pre-decision relative-yield potential as a genuine agronomic score. Winter
wheat ranks first (\ScoreWheat) but receives the least land (\PrimaryWheat),
below soybean (\PrimarySoy) and corn (\PrimaryCorn), in the
Student-\(t\) model. Reversal appears in \PhaseReversal{} of \PhaseCells{}
copula--dependence--risk cells; \PhaseComplete{} are selected complete rank
reversals. Strong exclusion is structurally inadmissible in this principal
specification because every crop has a positive minimum share. When all crop
lower bounds are relaxed to zero, \AllZeroStrong{} selected,
\AllZeroPossibleStrong{} possible and \AllZeroUniversalStrong{} universal
strong reversals occur on the same grid, a substantive null despite crop exit
being admissible. The Kansas inversion is margin-induced and is moderated by
risk. Separate controlled
experiments identify a risk-induced Soybean--Corn crossing with the
higher-score crop also having the higher mean margin, and an operational
crossing under a tightened rotation cap. A full-investment matched-Kendall
Selected Gaussian mean--variance policy chosen by a fixed
\DiversificationTarget\%
variance-reduction rule reduces variance relative to an expected-profit
benchmark yet violates the Student-\(t\) evaluation-law CVaR ceiling. Strong
failure holds over \(\gamma\in[\DiversificationStrongGammaInterval]\) in the
focal experiment; because the tail-aware optimum binds the ceiling, tail
inferiority and ceiling violation coincide numerically. In a two-stage
extension, adjacent-grid cross-differences under one shared ex-ante CVaR
ceiling are positive, zero or negative depending on what flexibility changes.
A separate
\StateYears{}-state-year public panel shows widespread score--acreage
disagreement but no leakage-free lagged association, so it validates
prevalence only descriptively. Ranking reversal is therefore a conditional
decision-system property, not a generic defect of ranking and not evidence
that observed acreage is privately optimal.
\end{abstract}
