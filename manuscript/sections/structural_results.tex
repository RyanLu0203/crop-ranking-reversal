\section{Ranking does not identify allocation}

A crop ranking and an acreage allocation are different mathematical objects. Let a suitability vector $s$ induce a weak order and let $x\in\mathbb{R}^n_+$ denote acreage. Strictly increasing transformations preserve the order in $s$ but can change any proportional score-to-acreage rule. The ranking also supplies neither an objective nor a feasible set. Consequently, even a strict order cannot identify a cardinal acreage vector.

The distinction becomes sharper when the optimum is set-valued. Let
\begin{equation}
X=\{x:\ell\leq x\leq u,\;\mathbf 1^\top x\leq A,\;c^\top x\leq B,\;Gx\leq g\}
\end{equation}
and let $r_\alpha(x)=\operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha[-\widetilde\pi^\top x]$ be loss-CVaR. The optimal face is
\begin{equation}
S_\kappa=\arg\max\{\mu^\top x:x\in X,\;r_\alpha(x)\leq\kappa\}.
\end{equation}
For a ranked pair $s_i>s_j$, reversal is \emph{possible} if $x_j>x_i$ at some $x\in S_\kappa$, \emph{universal} if it holds at every $x\in S_\kappa$, and \emph{selected} if it holds at the allocation returned by a declared deterministic rule. These definitions prevent one solver vertex from being mistaken for the entire optimal face.

Four exact two-crop constructions share the same coordinate system (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}). Cardinal margins can point against the external crop ranking. A bound or contract can place the entire feasible set in the reversal half-space. A binding downside-risk constraint can move the optimum without implying monotone crop-wise comparative statics. A flat optimal face can cross the rank boundary, making reversal possible but not universal. Identical-looking allocations can thus encode different claims.

Operational feasibility supplies a transparent universal certificate. If $s_i>s_j$ and
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x\in X}x_i+\tau_x<\inf_{x\in X}x_j,
\end{equation}
then every feasible allocation reverses the pair; $u_i+\tau_x<\ell_j$ is a simple sufficient condition. This result needs no downside-risk channel. Conversely, if an unconstrained expected-margin optimum is risk-feasible, the risk constraint leaves the optimal value unchanged, but one slack selected solution does not establish equality of optimal faces when optima are multiple.

The complete first-order identity blocks a second shortcut. At an optimum, non-negative multipliers and a subgradient $d\in\partial r_\alpha(x)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
0=-\mu+\lambda d+\gamma\mathbf 1+\beta c+G^\top\eta-a+b.
\end{equation}
For crops $i$ and $j$,
\begin{equation}
\mu_i-\mu_j=\lambda(d_i-d_j)+\beta(c_i-c_j)+(G^\top\eta)_i-(G^\top\eta)_j-(a_i-a_j)+(b_i-b_j).
\label{eq:kktpair}
\end{equation}
The common land multiplier cancels, whereas budget, shared-constraint and bound terms remain. Equation~\ref{eq:kktpair} describes local marginal balance; it does not rank acreage levels.

The closed M0--M4 path makes these distinctions auditable (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomposition}). Successive stages add cardinal margins, operations, downside risk and dependence to a common benchmark. Allocation and indexed outcome panels retain the actual registered outputs; an all-subset Shapley decomposition reports attribution intervals and closes with maximum efficiency residual $\ShapleyResidual$. Signed E2 pressure terms show how margin pressure is balanced locally by budget, shared or boundary terms, with maximum stationarity residual $\KKTResidual$. These are accounting decompositions over a controlled model, not causal effects in observed acreage.
