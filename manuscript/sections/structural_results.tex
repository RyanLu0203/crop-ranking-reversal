\section{Structural results and mechanism decomposition}

The first result is an identification theorem: a weak or strict suitability order alone does not determine a cardinal acreage vector. Strictly increasing transformations preserve the order but can change a proportional mapping, and neither transformation nor ranking supplies the objective, feasible set, risk convention or optimizer selection. Ordinal insufficiency is therefore structural rather than a numerical failure.

Existence and tractability follow under conventional conditions. If $X$ is non-empty and compact, per-acre profits are integrable, and the risk-feasible subset is non-empty, an optimizer exists. The risk-feasible subset is convex and the finite-scenario problem is linear. These assumptions retain the original multi-crop architecture while making its domain explicit.

Operational feasibility can force universal reversal without a risk mechanism. If $s_i>s_j$ and
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x\in X}x_i+\tau_x<\inf_{x\in X}x_j,
\end{equation}
then every feasible allocation reverses the pair; $u_i+\tau_x<\ell_j$ is a transparent sufficient certificate. Conversely, if an unconstrained expected-profit optimum is risk-feasible, the risk-constrained optimal value is unchanged and the constrained optimum is the intersection of the unconstrained optimal set with the risk-feasible set. One observed slack solution does not prove equality of solution sets when optima are multiple.

The complete first-order characterization blocks a second shortcut. At an optimum $x^*$, non-negative multipliers and a subgradient $d\in\partial r_\alpha(x^*)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
0=-\mu+\lambda d+\gamma\mathbf 1+\beta c+G^\top\eta-a+b,
\end{equation}
together with primal feasibility, dual feasibility and complementary slackness. For crops $i$ and $j$,
\begin{equation}
\mu_i-\mu_j=\lambda(d_i-d_j)+\beta(c_i-c_j)+(G^\top\eta)_i-(G^\top\eta)_j-(a_i-a_j)+(b_i-b_j).
\label{eq:kktpair}
\end{equation}
The common land multiplier cancels, whereas budget, shared-constraint and bound terms remain. Equation~\ref{eq:kktpair} describes local marginal balance; it does not rank acreage levels.

Dependence comparative statics require a distributional premise. If fixed marginals within a named family give $L_{\theta_1}(x)\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}L_{\theta_2}(x)$ for every allocation in a declared domain, loss-CVaR is weakly ordered there. When the domain is all of $X$, risk-feasible sets are nested and optimal expected profit is weakly non-increasing. No crop-specific acreage ordering follows without further uniqueness and selection assumptions. Similarly, possible and universal reversal regions defined by the maximum and minimum optimal-face acreage gaps may be empty, disconnected, multiple or interval-valued. A unique dependence threshold requires continuity, strict monotonicity and an endpoint crossing; it is not generic.

The nested M0--M4 audit makes these distinctions computationally inspectable (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomposition}). M0 begins with a closed benchmark; successive stages add margins, operational restrictions, downside risk and dependence. Across the closed-domain seeds, the operations block shifts corn share downward and soybean share upward, whereas dependence and risk contributions have intervals that cross zero for crop shares. The all-subset Shapley decomposition closes to a maximum efficiency residual of $\ShapleyResidual$. These are exact accounting contributions over registered block orders, not causal effects.

The local pressure audit then evaluates Eq.~\ref{eq:kktpair} in E2. Margin pressure is balanced by budget, shared-constraint or boundary terms according to the intervention class, while the tail-risk term is zero in those cells. The maximum registered stationarity residual is $\KKTResidual$. This connects the theorem to a positive operational mechanism without attributing acreage shares to an omitted private farm process. Full proofs, assumptions, optimal-face constructions and order-sensitivity ranges are in the Supplementary Information.
