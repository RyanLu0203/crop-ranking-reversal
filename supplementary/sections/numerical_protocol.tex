\section{Confirmatory simulation protocol and complete evidence ledger}

\subsection{Design and precision rule}
The frozen E1--E6 design specifies factors, estimands, multiplicity, sequential checks, precision targets and maximum replication counts. An experiment meets the criterion only when every registered primary interval passes. E2 and E6 stop with precision achieved at $n=\EtwoReplications{}$ and $n=\EsixReplications{}$, respectively. E1, E3, E4 and E5 reach $n=64$ without meeting the complete experiment-level criterion. Their signs and isolated passing contrasts are not promoted.

\subsection{E1 cardinal-margin experiment}
E1 tests margin-driven reversal around registered gap contrasts. Its allocation contrasts are stable in sign, but the experiment-level criterion fails at the ceiling. Figure~\ref{fig:s1} preserves the interval geometry and the decision ledger.

\subsection{E4 and E5 dependence experiments}
E4 tests dependence intensity within named families; E5 evaluates policies under paired true and assumed laws. Neither experiment meets its complete precision criterion. The cross-law pattern is retained as a diagnostic and cannot identify a universal dependence threshold or misspecification penalty.

\subsection{Numerical integrity and attribution order}
Reverse-order replay passes \ReplayPasses{}/\ReplayTotal{} checks and solver-method sensitivity passes \SolverPasses{}/\SolverTotal{}. The maximum KKT stationarity residual is $\KKTResidual$ and the maximum Shapley efficiency residual is $\ShapleyResidual$. The ledger includes all \RegisteredInfeasible{} registered infeasible rows. Order-path ranges expose the dependence of sequential attribution on block order; the all-subset Shapley value is an accounting average rather than a causal decomposition.
