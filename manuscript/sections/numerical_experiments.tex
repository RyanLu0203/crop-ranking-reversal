\section{Numerical experiments}

The numerical experiment probes the repaired propositions rather than calibrating a representative farmer. A preregistered mixed-factor design combines named copula and marginal families with Kendall dependence, a CVaR frontier quantile, budget tightness, a dominant-crop cap and a contract minimum. It contains \SimulationCells{} cells and five fixed replications per cell, yielding \SimulationReplications{} primary optimizations. Parameters not identified by public aggregate data are labelled illustrative and are varied over frozen ranges.

Every primary solve reached an optimal status. Reverse-order independent replay passed for \ReplayPasses{} replications, and all \SolverPasses{} solver-method comparisons passed. Direct CVaR evaluation, tail-weight identities and KKT residuals were checked against frozen tolerances. These results support implementation integrity, not the substantive prevalence of reversal.

The substantive landscape is deliberately supplementary. Universal reversal occurs in \UniversalReplications{} replications; the CVaR constraint binds in \RiskBindingReplications{} and is slack in \RiskSlackReplications{}; and the preregistered pseudo-diversification diagnostic is flagged in \PseudoDiversificationReplications{}. These variations refute any blanket assertion that reversal requires a binding CVaR constraint or that dependence produces one monotone threshold.

Most importantly, the convergence audit fails. Across \ConvergenceRows{} scenario-count rows, only \ConvergencePassRows{} satisfy the joint numeric-pass and interval-width rule; the largest Wilson interval width remains \ConvergenceWidth{}. The simulation figures and substantive counts are therefore marked \textsc{nonheadline}. We report this adverse result because passing solver diagnostics and failing inferential precision are different facts.
