\begin{abstract}
Crop rankings reduce heterogeneous evidence to an order, whereas land allocation is a cardinal decision made under joint uncertainty and operational restrictions. We formalize this gap with a set-valued, multi-crop programme that maximizes expected profit subject to a polyhedral feasible set and loss-based conditional value at risk. The theory distinguishes possible, universal and selected reversal, proves that rankings alone cannot identify acreage, and shows that operational feasibility can force reversal even when downside risk is inactive. In preregistered simulations, operational interventions convert a corn-only solution into soybean-majority allocations in all treated E2 cells; all \EtwoPassedIntervals{}/\EtwoIntervals{} family-wise intervals meet their precision targets. A separate E6 experiment shows that the interaction between information and flexibility can be positive (\EsixPositive{}, 95\% interval \EsixPositiveLow{} to \EsixPositiveHigh{}), exactly null, or substitutive, establishing actionability without universal complementarity. Four other experiments fail their registered precision gates and remain adverse evidence. In admitted US state data, concurrent operating-margin--acreage inversion intensity is \OperatingInversion{} (state-cluster 95\% interval \OperatingInversionLow{} to \OperatingInversionHigh{}), but all four lagged share-change intervals include zero and national results depend on aggregation. Thus ranking reversal is theoretically coherent and reproducible under explicit mechanisms, while observed acreage disagreement alone does not identify private constraints, downside-risk preferences, dependence, causality or welfare.
\end{abstract}
