\section{Robustness and extensions}

Three robustness layers are kept distinct. Leave-one-state-out and year-specific summaries test whether the concurrent inversion intensity is mechanically driven by a single cluster or crop year. Rolling-origin transitions impose temporal ordering but remain descriptive because past public scores are not farmer beliefs. National aggregation changes the unit of analysis and, for relative yield, creates ties by construction. None of these checks supplies the missing private feasible set.

The simulation has an equally explicit robustness hierarchy. Numerical replay, solver-method agreement, KKT closure and Shapley efficiency are implementation checks. Registered intervals determine inferential precision. The E2 result is additionally robust to separate and combined budget, rotation, contract and corn-bound interventions within the frozen cell family. E6 includes constructive positive, exact-null and substitution cases, so its conclusion does not rest on assuming that every added action is useful in every information state.

The framework can accommodate stochastic costs, fixed charges, endogenous prices, recourse after information, spatial coupling and multi-period rotations. Each extension changes the decision problem and requires new assumptions or data. In particular, a dynamic acreage model could separate physical rotation from adjustment inertia, while farm-level observations on expectations and contracts could connect controlled operational mechanisms to field decisions. Named dependence laws could be compared under verified portfolio-loss orders, but the present failed E4/E5 gates do not authorize a general dependence claim.
